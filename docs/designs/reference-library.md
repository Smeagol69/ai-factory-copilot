# The reference blueprint library — what finished builds are actually made of

Eight human-authored builds were decoded and measured: a matched set of six
production modules, one purely architectural piece, and one finished home base.
Every number below came out of the pinned read-only serializer via
`inspectBlueprintStructure`, or out of the interactive-map export. None of it is
recalled, and none of it is estimated.

The catalog lives in `companion/lib/blueprint-reference-catalog.mjs`, generated by
`scripts/ingest-blueprint-reference.mjs`. The model reads it through
`find_reference_designs`.

## The measurement that matters

Across the seven designer blueprints — 890 placed buildings in total:

| Role | Count | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| enclosure | 567 | 63.7% |
| logistics | 143 | 16.1% |
| signage | 72 | 8.1% |
| power | 63 | 7.1% |
| **production** | **24** | **2.7%** |
| access | 21 | 2.4% |

**Machines are 2.7% of a production module.** Twenty-four of them, across six
modules, surrounded by 567 pieces of foundation, wall, beam and pillar.

That is the whole answer to why generated designs read as boxes. A planner that
emits machines and belts is not building a simplified version of these
references — it is building a different category of object and stopping at the
point where the human work starts. The generated result is not 90% of a
reference design; on this measure it is 19%.

Include the home base and the pattern gets stronger, not weaker: 7,347 buildings
across the library, of which 34 are machines (0.5%) and 4,859 are enclosure
(66.1%).

## The six modules share two shells

The censuses give this away:

| Design | Envelope | Buildings | Machines | Enclosure | Belt pairs |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 120 Iron Ingot | 6×6×6 | 99 | 4 | 57 | 32 |
| 80 Iron Plate | 6×6×6 | 107 | 4 | 70 | 27 |
| 120 Screw | 6×6×6 | 107 | 4 | 70 | 27 |
| 120 Iron Rod | 6×6×6 | 155 | 8 | 88 | 55 |
| 10 R. Iron Plate | 6×6×6 | 122 | 2 | 92 | 19 |
| 8 Rotor | 6×6×6 | 122 | 2 | 92 | 19 |

**80 Iron Plate and 120 Screw are census-identical.** So are 10 Reinforced Iron
Plate and 8 Rotor. Same envelope, same building counts, same class mix, same belt
topology — different recipe. The author did not design six factories; they
designed a four-constructor shell and a two-assembler shell and re-dressed them.

Every module is 6×6×6, which is what makes a set. A generator that sizes each
module to its own contents produces six differently-shaped things that will never
line up. **Uniform envelope is a design decision, and it is the one that makes
modules tile.**

The Rod module is the outlier worth noting: double the machines and nearly double
the belt pairs of its siblings, in the same envelope. Density varies; the
envelope does not.

## The parts real builders reach for

Ranked by how many separate designs use a part, which is a better idiomaticity
signal than raw count — one design placing 154 walls should not outvote a part
that five designs each use:

- `Wall_Concrete_8x4`, `Foundation_Concrete_8x1`, `Wall_Concrete_8x1` — the shell
- `Wall_Concrete_Tris_8x8` / `Wall_Concrete_FlipTris_8x8` — **triangular walls, 67
  of them.** These close gables and corners. Nothing in the current generator can
  emit a non-rectangular wall, which is a large part of why output reads as
  boxes.
- `Wall_Concrete_8x4_ConveyorHole_01` — a belt passes through a wall through a
  purpose-made hole, rather than the wall stopping short
- `Beam_Painted`, `Beam_Connector`, `Pillar_Small_Concrete` — exposed structure
- `Catwalk*` (straight, corner, T, cross, stairs) — service access at height
- `StandaloneWidgetSign_*` and `SignPole_*` — 72 signs across seven modules;
  labelling is part of the build, not an afterthought
- `Wall_Concrete_SDoor_8x4`, `Gate_Automated_8x4` — you can walk in

The `[ARCH] Miner Wrap` is the clearest single lesson in the set: **178 buildings,
zero machines.** It is an 8×8×8 architectural jacket that dresses a miner someone
else already placed. Pure enclosure and detail, packaged as a reusable unit. It
proves the category exists and is worth generating on its own.

## The home base, at scale

`The HUB Home Base` is a `.cbp` interactive-map export, not a designer blueprint —
a flat list of actors with absolute world transforms. 6,457 buildings plus 2,936
placed-blueprint proxies, spanning **29.0 × 44.3 foundation cells over about 19
floor levels**.

Two things carried over from it:

**It is assembled from blueprints.** 2,936 proxies against 6,457 loose buildings
means roughly a third of what is there was placed as a pre-made unit. The
large-scale workflow is compose-from-modules, not place-every-wall — which is
exactly the workflow the Architect should be emitting.

**Colour is used deliberately.** 1,582 actors carry a custom swatch, 980 use
palette Slot 0, and 697 carry a Carbon Steel paint finish against 615 left
unpainted. Fewer than half of all surfaces are default. The catalog records the
palette histogram per world export.

One measurement caveat, recorded because it nearly became a wrong number: taking
the naive min/max over every actor in that file gives a 250 × 384 cell footprint.
That is an artifact. Two `FGDroneStationInfo` marker actors sit at world origin
while the base itself is at roughly (−183907, 281043), and those two records alone
stretch the extent tenfold. The ingest counts only actors whose class path carries
the native `/Build_` convention — the same test the blueprint reader applies — and
reports proxies and non-buildable actors separately. One real building, a
`BP_ElevatorCabin`, does not carry that convention and is excluded by it; that is a
known undercount of one, kept in preference to a fuzzier rule.

## What this library is not

Declared inputs and outputs are parsed from the author's own description text
("Input: 120 Iron ore"). They are **author claims**, carried through the catalog
labelled as such. They are not decoded, not simulated, and not verified against
any recipe. A rate used for planning still comes from `content.recipes` and the
live snapshot, as always.

Nothing here proves terrain fit, hologram validity, unlock state, or that any of
these designs can be built at a given site. The catalog measures other people's
finished work. It is evidence about proportion, vocabulary, and envelope — not
evidence about this save.

## Sources

The binaries are not committed. They are other people's creative work and this
repository is public, so `reference/blueprints/sources/` is gitignored and only
the derived measurements ship. `reference/blueprints/sources.json` records what
each entry is and where it came from; drop the files back into `sources/` and
re-run the ingest to reproduce the catalog byte-for-byte
(`node scripts/ingest-blueprint-reference.mjs --check` verifies it is current).

The six production modules are credited to **Skyskiner** in their own `.sbpcfg`
metadata.
